INDIA · 2026

Inflation isn't the same everywhere.

by Diyana Soni

The cost of living has moved differently across states and between rural and urban households. The gap has also changed direction over time.

Hover or tap a state to see combined, rural and urban inflation.

Source: NSO, MoSPI, Consumer Price Index for July 2026, released Aug. 12, 2026. Primary release

01 · HIGHEST COMBINED INFLATION

Telangana is where prices are rising fastest.

Telangana recorded the highest combined inflation in the July 2026 state data, at 6.32%. Rural inflation was 6.86%, while urban inflation was 5.92%. The New Indian Express reported that Telangana had recorded the highest state inflation every month since January, with economists linking part of the rise to the introduction of the new CPI series. The New Indian Express

In June, prices of edible oils in Hyderabad had risen by ₹20–30 a litre over three months, while tomatoes and several other vegetables were selling at ₹80–90 a kilogram, according to The New Indian Express.

Source: NSO, MoSPI, Consumer Price Index for July 2026, released Aug. 12, 2026; Telangana context from The New Indian Express, June–August 2026. Primary release

02 · LOWEST COMBINED INFLATION

At the other end, prices barely moved, Mizoram.

Mizoram recorded the lowest combined inflation in the July 2026 dataset, at 1.84%. Rural inflation was 2.27%, compared with 1.52% in urban areas. The state had also recorded a sharp reduction in inflation earlier in the decade. Mizoram's Economic Survey 2024-25 reported combined inflation of 3.29% in December 2023, down from 8.41% a year earlier. Economic Survey of Mizoram 2024-25

Source: NSO, MoSPI, Consumer Price Index for July 2026; Economic Survey of Mizoram 2024-25, Government of Mizoram. Primary release

03 · RURAL VS URBAN

Rural and urban prices do not move together.

In July 2026, all-India inflation was 4.45%. Rural inflation was 4.84%, compared with 3.96% in urban areas. Food inflation was 5.79% in rural areas and 5.05% in urban areas.

Across the state data, the rural-urban difference moved in both directions. The figures ranged from a 1.73 percentage-point rural lead in Andaman and Nicobar Islands to a 1.18 percentage-point urban lead in Himachal Pradesh.

Source: NSO, MoSPI, Consumer Price Index for July 2026, Aug. 12, 2026; author's calculations from state-level data. Primary release

04 · LARGEST RURAL-OVER-URBAN GAP

Andaman & Nicobar: +1.73 pp.

Rural inflation in Andaman and Nicobar Islands was 4.99%, compared with 3.26% in urban areas, giving a gap of 1.73 percentage points.

The islands' supply chain is heavily dependent on maritime cargo. The government's LEADS 2025 report identifies port infrastructure and cargo movement as key parts of essential-commodity logistics in the islands. A 2026 Budget proposal for a Coastal Cargo Promotion Scheme was described as a measure expected to reduce logistics costs and stabilise prices of essential commodities brought from the mainland.

Source: NSO, MoSPI, Consumer Price Index for July 2026; DPIIT, Logistics Ease Across Different States (LEADS) 2025; Andaman and Nicobar Administration, 2026 Budget-related release. Primary release

05 · LARGEST URBAN-OVER-RURAL GAP

Himachal Pradesh flips it: −1.18 pp.

Himachal Pradesh recorded rural inflation of 3.45% and urban inflation of 4.63%, producing the largest urban-over-rural gap in the dataset at 1.18 percentage points.

Himachal Pradesh's Economic Survey 2025-26 reported that transport inflation had reached 6.9% in 2025, while health inflation remained elevated. The survey identified transport and health as major contributors to inflation during the year, while housing and clothing inflation remained relatively subdued.

Source: NSO, MoSPI, Consumer Price Index for July 2026; Government of Himachal Pradesh, Economic Survey 2025-26. Primary release

The split across 35 states and UTs.

Of the 35 states and Union Territories with both rural and urban inflation figures, 27 recorded higher rural inflation, 7 recorded higher urban inflation and 1 recorded equal inflation.

Nagaland recorded 2.82% in both rural and urban areas. Chandigarh is not included in the comparison because the July 2026 dataset has no rural inflation figure for it.

Source: NSO, MoSPI, Consumer Price Index for July 2026; author's calculation from state-level data. Primary release

07 · STATE-BY-STATE GAP

Where the numbers spread out.

The median rural-minus-urban gap across the 35 comparable states and UTs was +0.92 percentage points.

The full range was 2.91 percentage points, from −1.18 pp in Himachal Pradesh to +1.73 pp in Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

Source: NSO, MoSPI, Consumer Price Index for July 2026; author's calculations from state-level data. Primary release

STEP BACK

Across comparable states, rural inflation averages higher.

Taken together, the comparable states have average rural inflation of 4.27%, against 3.60% for urban areas. That is a gap of about 0.67 percentage points. But this is an average across states, not a measure of what the average Indian household pays. It simply captures the direction seen across the state-level observations, with substantial differences hidden underneath the two numbers.

09 · THE GAP

The rural–urban gap changed direction 21 times.

The rural-minus-urban inflation gap changed sign 21 times in the monthly series used for the analysis.

The gap was positive in 2016, negative in 2019, and negative again in 2025.

Source: NSO, MoSPI CPI monthly series; author's calculation of the rural-minus-urban gap and monthly sign changes.

10 · 2016

Rural pulls ahead: +1.40 pp.

The annual rural-minus-urban inflation gap was +1.40 percentage points in 2016.

In July 2016, all-India rural inflation was 6.20%, compared with 5.26% in urban areas.

Source: NSO, MoSPI CPI series; author's calculation of the annual rural-urban gap. July 2016 comparison from MoSPI CPI release.

11 · 2019

Urban takes the lead: −1.67 pp.

The annual rural-minus-urban gap was −1.67 percentage points in 2019.

By December, all-India urban headline inflation was 7.46%, compared with 7.26% in rural areas. The urban-rural difference was wider in food inflation: 16.12% in urban areas versus 12.97% in rural areas.

Source: NSO, MoSPI, CPI December 2019 release; author's calculation of the annual 2019 rural-urban gap. December 2019 release

12 · 2025

The gap moved back below zero: −0.58 pp.

The annual rural-minus-urban gap was −0.58 percentage points in 2025.

The Economic Survey 2025-26, Ministry of Finance, says rural inflation had remained above urban inflation through much of 2023 and 2024, but moved below urban inflation during 2025 as food inflation eased. The survey also shows rural inflation ending 2025 below urban inflation in its monthly series.

Source: NSO, MoSPI CPI series; author's calculation of the 2025 annual gap. Context: Government of India, Economic Survey 2025-26, Chapter 5 — Inflation: Tamed and Anchored. Economic Survey

13 · OPEN THE BASKET

The gap moves when different parts of the basket move.

In 2018-19, rural consumer food price inflation was 2.67 percentage points lower than urban inflation.

For the broader Food & Beverages group, the rural-minus-urban gap was −2.30 percentage points.

The difference shows that the rural-urban inflation gap can change even inside the food-heavy part of the CPI basket.

Source: Author's analysis of NSO/MoSPI CPI subgroup data, 2018-19.

THE TAKEAWAY

The gap has moved both ways.

In July 2026, rural inflation was higher than urban inflation in 27 of 35 comparable states and UTs. The state-level gap ranged from +1.73 percentage points to −1.18 percentage points, while the equal-weighted state average was 4.27% for rural inflation and 3.62% for urban inflation.

From 2014 to 2025, combined inflation ranged from 2.23% to 6.71%, while the rural-urban gap changed direction repeatedly.

Source: NSO, MoSPI CPI series; author's calculations from state-level and monthly data.
